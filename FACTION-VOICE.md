# Camonna Tong: designing a faction voice

## The brief

Give the faction a recognisable criminal voice while keeping dialogue readable, meaningful and native to Morrowind.

The creative direction drew on:

- **Baltimore, through The Wire:** the feel of street hierarchy, working relationships, loyalty and practical criminal business.
- **Birmingham, through Peaky Blinders:** intimidation, status, terse bargaining and organised-crew identity.
- **Kingdom Come: Deliverance 1 and 2:** coarse, grounded bandit speech that helps keep the register away from modern urban technology.

Those descriptions explain the editorial intention, not linguistic research findings. These are mediated fictional references, not representative samples of the communities' real speech. “A fusion of criminal registers” is more accurate than “a faithful Baltimore–Birmingham dialect.”

## Constraints that mattered more than style

1. Preserve the Russian line's meaning, quest information and conditions.
2. Keep the exact English topic phrases required for dialogue links.
3. Retain established in-world vocabulary: n'wah, outlander, skooma and drakes.
4. Use **crew** consistently for the faction's criminal grouping; do not alternate casually with gang.
5. Avoid catchphrases, recognisable quotations and direct references to the shows.
6. Do not introduce modern technology or real-world locations.
7. Use register, vocabulary and rhythm; avoid blanket phonetic spelling.
8. Treat official English Morrowind usage as the terminology reference. For ambiguous lore terms, consult the relevant original lore writing.

## How the pass was organised

The faction pass used 618 keyed entries identified through faction/speaker metadata and related scripts or topics. Keeping stable keys let editorial rewrites override a model draft without changing record identity.

Each rewrite had two separate questions:

- Does it sound like the faction?
- Does it still do the same job in the game?

The submission tooling checked required topic names and variable preservation. Warnings still needed review; the presence of a checker did not guarantee correctness. Some flags were accepted deliberately, so this was an assisted editorial process rather than an infallible gate.

## A gameplay lesson from language

A later Camonna Tong quest stalled because one gender-specific dialogue variant used a fluent alternative to **spread a rumor**. That wording no longer taught or linked the expected topic. The correction restored the required phrase while leaving the record identifier intact.

A line can be grammatically good and stylistically convincing, yet still be a localisation bug. The topic phrase is both prose and a functional interface.

## What is deliberately absent here

This repository does not reproduce the full source or translated dialogue, and it does not invent before/after examples and present them as shipped lines. The portfolio evidence is the brief, the pipeline, the documented review scope and the concrete failure analysis.
